Course Goals


1. Consider the role of rhetoric, technology, and ethics in acts of making.

I've been approaching this topic from two angles--one in this class, and another in my Mass Communication & Society class. Using Adobe definitely made me reflect on the tools used, I had rarely ventured outside of a few free programs before this class. The UX Research Project made me consider the viewer first: where the viewer would look, how they would take in information, etc. The technical writing aspect of the Good Design project made me think about balancing understandability with design--something I had already considered in regard to accessible design, but haven't implemented as thoughtfully before. Design Fuego made me reflect on what I was already bringing to the class and what I was hoping to learn, but other projects have expanded my thoughts from those already.

2. Leverage Design Principles.

The A/B/C Logo project, alongside the Good Design project, helped me effectively utilize and implement the major design principles in my own work. While I already had theoretical knowledge, learning how to use them in an independent setting made me feel more comfortable trying new things. The revision aspect especially helped me understand what went wrong, while the knowledge of design principles helped me understand why, and subsequently how I could fix it. Already, I'm implementing contrast and proximity in a redesign of my résumé.

3. Engage with others in a workshop environment.

The revision-based projects, Logo A/B/C and Good Design, gave me good experince in engaging with others. It made me appreciate the fresh perspectives that others could give, having seen my design for the first time. It also trained me to analyze design more critically, although I feel I have a ways to go in putting my feelings to words and actionable information. Understanding and having worked with the design principles, however, has already given me new language for why something feels a certain way, and helps me both provide feedback and use others' feedback.



4. Practice revision within and across media forms.

Logo A/B/C and Good Design really honed in on this for me. While I've typically been averse to revision in visual art, instead focusing on what I can do better in a later piece, both projects have made me approach visual design the way I do writing--worthy of multiple passes in order to bring out the best version. It also connects well to the engaging with others in a workshop environment bit--I would likely be stuck on what to revise without fresh feedback.

5. Learn to present your work.

Projects like the Logo UX Research Project slideshow, Design Fuego, and Good Design gave me practice in designing something approachable through a speaker-present presentation, or independent viewing and reading (portable). It was most obvious in the slideshow--I got to pack the most information into the space, while trying to keep in mind the design principles, and how I would present it later. I found the contrast between the UX research project, where I was presenting logos with little information, and the Design Fuego project, where I was presenting a lot of information condensed, very interesting. It made me reflect more on the utility of design, and how something could be seen with and without accompaniment.

Accessible Design

An accessible PDF, including tagged elements and alt text.

Course Goals


1. Consider the role of rhetoric, technology, and ethics in acts of making.

Ethics and technology were a big part of the accessibility project. Rhetoric was more so the magazine and infographic, although everything was present in everything, of course. The very idea of a tagged PDF had not occurred to me, even when I already have difficulty using assistive technology with PDFs myself, and that highlighted the many kinds of technology that are involved in the process of design that I was unaware of. Ethics also made one ask: who are you excluding with this design, and how can you fix that?

2. Leverage Design Principles.

I think for these projects, I especially learned how to leverage the design principles in a way that would make content more accessible and understandable. This connected to the focus on technical writing and made me understand how much a good page layout aided the understanding of the contents, even when the contents were already well written. The two-page spread and magazine project helped me think about the aesthetic and practical value of the design principles, notably alignment, proximity, and contrast. Learning about type also helped strengthen my understanding of contrast, as seen in the magazine project.

3. Engage with others in a workshop environment.

These projects mainly helped me in being better about presenting and sharing my work, although it also changed my mindset a bit in the sense that I was coming to class with an expectation of having my work slightly criticized or prodded. I also learned from others--having other material to look at and understanding the ways others approach projects has been very refreshing. I found the various magazine pages and accessible PDFs particularly interesting. It made me reconsider my workflow as starting from image and building off, rather than using an image supplementally, as, while I enjoyed working that way in the magazine and infographic project, it had not occurred to me in the accessible PDF project.



4. Practice revision within and across media forms.

I think, even if not exactly revision, these projects made me more intentional with my choices, and made me tinker with more than just the program's (InDesign's) automatic settings. The kerning in the magazine project, for example, started off as automatic spacing, but I tinkered with it until it felt even more right, when before I likely would not have touched it. The magazine project also made me revise my technical writing for clarity, but also working within the design constraints--eliminating orphans and widows being a challenge.

5. Learn to present your work.

With regard to presentation specifically, I think most of these projects made me realize that I have difficulty understanding where to start presenting my work--as in, what part should be talked about first. To me, that connects to the accessible PDF project especially, since part of accessibility is making the starting point and flow clear and comprehensible. It also reminds me of the portable slideshow part of the Design Beginnings section, which I believe I've taken some of my thoughts with me through to the design brief I've been working on.
